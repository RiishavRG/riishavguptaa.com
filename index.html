About

I'm a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC), where I work in the Future Sensing and Interaction (FSI) Lab with Dr. Dong Li. My research develops smartphone-based methods for cardiovascular health sensing, combining digital signal processing, acoustic sensing, and machine learning to bring meaningful health insights to everyday devices.

Before my Ph.D., I spent over five years as a senior iOS engineer at Gojek and Nuclei, building mobile apps used by millions of people. I'm especially interested in research that bridges low-level sensing with real-world, deployable mobile systems that reach the people who need them.

You may also find my work published under the name Riishav Guptaa.

Research

One idea connects my projects: a phone's speaker and microphone can work as an ultrasonic sensor, with no extra hardware.

  • Measuring blood flow through the skin

    Phonotonos recovers arterial blood-flow velocity waveforms with phase-based ultrasonic Doppler on a commodity smartphone, fusing ultrasound with arterial sound and motion sensing to find the artery and reject hand motion. It measures four clinical Doppler indices with accuracy comparable to dedicated medical devices.

    • MobiSys '26 paper
    • MobiCom '26 demo
    • Code and sample data on GitHub

  • Reading tissue mechanics with sound

    Body-contact devices such as haptic wristbands, exoskeletons, and prosthetics press on soft tissue that differs from person to person. I estimate tissue relaxation time from acoustic chirps and their echoes using a phone's built-in speaker and microphone, a first step toward devices that tune themselves to each wearer.

    • MobiCom '26 work-in-progress paper

  • Securing phone calls with ultrasound

    AI voice cloning lets scammers impersonate trusted people on calls. Ultrasound Watermark binds live call audio to the caller's articulatory motion (mouth, jaw, and cheek movements sensed with the phone's own ultrasound), which makes the watermark hard to fake.

    • EdgeSP '26 paper

I work with Dr. Dong Li at UMBC and with collaborators at UMass Amherst, Nanyang Technological University, Indiana University, and Amazon.

  1. Work in progressTo appear

    Smartphone-based Acoustic Tissue Characterization for Personalized Body-contact Systems

    Rishav Gupta, Alyssa N. Maguina, Sithumina Weerarathna, Janelle P. Clark, Dong Li

    ACM MobiCom 2026 · 32nd International Conference on Mobile Computing and Networking, work-in-progress track · Austin, TX

    • MobiCom 2026 conference site
    Abstract

    Interactive systems such as haptic wristbands maintain sustained body contact, but variations in soft tissue mechanics can cause the same force to feel different across users. Conventional tissue characterization relies on specialized systems such as ultrasound elastography, limiting its use in personalized devices. We take a first step toward smartphone-based acoustic tissue characterization by investigating if a smartphone can estimate tissue relaxation time using its built-in speaker and microphone. The smartphone probes tissue with acoustic chirps and estimates relaxation behavior from tissue echoes. We validate the approach against laboratory indentation, observing a strong correlation between smartphone and mechanical estimates on a silicone phantom (r=0.96) and a weaker positive correlation on the human forearm (r=0.29). These preliminary results support the feasibility of smartphone-based acoustic tissue characterization for tissue-aware body-contact systems.

  2. DemoTo appear

    Phonotonos: Through-Skin Ultrasonic Blood Flow Sensing Using Smartphones

    Rishav Gupta, Shirui Cao, Jie Xiong, Sunghoon Ivan Lee, Jeremy Gummeson, Dong Li

    ACM MobiCom 2026 · 32nd International Conference on Mobile Computing and Networking, demo track · Austin, TX

    • Phonotonos code on GitHub
    • MobiCom 2026 conference site
    Abstract

    Cardiovascular diseases remain a leading cause of death worldwide, motivating accessible tools for vascular health monitoring. This demo presents Phonotonos, a smartphone-based system for through-skin ultrasonic sensing of blood-flow velocity waveforms and Doppler indices. Phonotonos repurposes smartphones for vascular sensing despite their low ultrasound transmit power, coarse spatial resolution, and lack of beamforming. The system combines phase-based ultrasonic Doppler sensing with arterial heart sounds and inertial measurements to localize arteries, mitigate motion interference, and recover blood-flow waveforms. Our demonstration showcases an end-to-end prototype in which users position a smartphone over an artery, receive real-time placement guidance, observe the recovered blood-flow waveform, and obtain clinically relevant Doppler indices. Through this interactive demo, we highlight the feasibility of transforming smartphones into portable and accessible tools for everyday vascular screening and monitoring.

    Ultrasound Watermark: Real-time Acoustic Watermarking against Voice Scams on Smartphones

    Renzhi Hao, Shirui Cao, Yizhu Wen, Rishav Gupta, Dharani Nadendla, Mehran Kafai, Hanqing Guo, Dong Li

    ACM/IEEE EdgeSP 2026 · 9th Workshop on Security and Privacy in Edge Computing, co-located with ACM/IEEE SEC 2026 · Santa Clara, CA

    • EdgeSP 2026 workshop site
    Abstract (excerpt)

    In this paper, we present Ultrasound Watermark, a real-time, smartphone-native framework for protecting live phone calls against voice impersonation attacks. Recent advances in text-to-speech (TTS), voice conversion (VC), and other AI-driven speech synthesis attacks enable adversaries to convincingly mimic trusted individuals during phone calls, creating a growing threat of voice scams. To defend, we introduce a real-time acoustic watermarking system that binds speech to a caller's physical articulatory motion. During a call, the caller's smartphone continuously emits ultrasonic signals and captures reflections from mouth, jaw, and cheek movements using only the built-in speaker and microphone. …

  4. ConferenceEmerging Ideas

    Phonotonos: Through-Skin Ultrasonic Blood Flow Sensing Using Smartphones

    Shirui Cao, Jie Xiong, Riishav Guptaa, Sunghoon Ivan Lee, Jeremy Gummeson, Dong Li

    ACM MobiSys 2026 · Proceedings of the 24th Annual International Conference on Mobile Systems, Applications and Services, pp. 911–927

    • Read the paper (DOI 10.1145/3745756.3809246)
    • Code and sample data on GitHub
    Abstract

    Cardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of death worldwide, and Doppler blood flow indices play a crucial role in their early detection and diagnosis. Existing ultrasound devices, though effective, are costly, bulky, and unsuitable for daily or at-home use. In this work, we introduce Phonotonos, a system that transforms commodity smartphones into portable and accessible tools for through-skin ultrasonic sensing of blood flow velocity. We demonstrate that despite smartphones' inherent limitations—including low ultrasound power, coarse spatial resolution, and lack of beamforming—blood flow velocity waveforms can still be recovered using novel phase-based signal processing, including nonlinearity cancellation and baseline drift removal. We further propose a triple-modality framework that fuses Doppler ultrasound, arterial sound, and IMU for robust artery localization and interfering motion (e.g., involuntary hand motion) rejection. Extensive simulations, phantom experiments, and IRB-approved human studies validate that our proposed system can measure four key Doppler indices (AT, S/D ratio, RI, PI) with accuracy comparable to dedicated medical devices. These results highlight the potential of smartphones to democratize vascular health monitoring and enable continuous cardiovascular screening in everyday environments.
